QUICK VIVA BACKUP – GROUP 1

1. Why did you choose this dataset?
It is a real-world road safety dataset with state-wise collision rates, driver behaviour percentages and insurance variables. It supports geographical and numerical analysis.

2. How many records and attributes?
51 records and 8 original attributes.

3. Missing values or duplicates?
No. Missing values = 0 and duplicate rows = 0.

4. Is there a time variable?
No. This is a cross-sectional state-level dataset. State is the geographical variable.

5. Which visualization answers which question?
Q1 geographical pattern → choropleth map.
Q2 top states → sorted horizontal bar chart.
Q3 alcohol vs total rate → scatter plot with trend line.
Q4 regional spread → box plot.
Q5 relationships between numerical variables → correlation heatmap.

6. Why not use a pie chart for state collision rates?
Because these rates are not parts of one total. Pie slices would suggest a whole that does not actually exist and make comparison difficult.

7. Main finding?
North Dakota and South Carolina have the highest fatal-collision rate in the dataset at 23.9. District of Columbia has the lowest at 5.9.

8. Does alcohol cause more fatal collisions according to your dashboard?
We cannot say that. In this dataset, alcohol-impaired percentage has only a weak positive correlation with total fatal-collision rate, about 0.19. Correlation does not prove causation.

9. What did AI suggest?
Add a trend line and hover labels to the scatter plot and avoid causal wording. We partially accepted it.

10. Why Streamlit?
It is simple for Python projects, supports interactive controls and works well with Plotly charts.

11. What happens when a filter is changed?
The dashboard creates a filtered dataframe and all KPI values and charts are generated from that filtered data.

12. Data source?
FiveThirtyEight bad-drivers dataset:
https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/bad-drivers
